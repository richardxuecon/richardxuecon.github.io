# Suggested paper summaries for the academic CV

These paragraphs follow the supplied CV and the accompanying website brief. They are intended to sit directly beneath the corresponding paper titles. Conference acceptances are separate from journal publication status.

## Expectation Sentiment and Consumption Fluctuation from the Covid Era: A Natural Language Processing Approach

This paper studies how economic expectations shaped U.S. consumer spending during the COVID-19 pandemic. I construct topic-specific sentiment measures from 1.2 million news articles and combine them with daily transaction data. A regression discontinuity design around Pfizer’s vaccine announcement identifies a 3.2% increase in spending, concentrated in groceries and food delivery. Vector autoregressive analysis and comparisons across income groups reveal distinct roles for public health, income, and unemployment expectations in consumption behavior.

## Leveraging ChatGPT to Analyze Heterogeneous Treatment Effects: The Effect of Pfizer’s COVID-19 Vaccine Announcement on U.S. Consumption

This paper develops a framework that uses embeddings of ChatGPT-generated text to identify sources of heterogeneous treatment effects beyond pre-specified numerical indicators. Applying a regression discontinuity in time design around Pfizer’s vaccine efficacy announcement, I examine variation in weekly consumption responses across U.S. cities. Cities with higher population density, higher income, and weaker healthcare infrastructure show stronger positive responses. The framework illustrates how language-model-derived textual information can complement conventional approaches to heterogeneity in applied causal inference.

## Balancing Act: A Multi-Stakeholder Analysis of IMF Perceptions Using LLMs

This project examines perceptions of the IMF’s 2018 Exceptional Access Program in Argentina among domestic publics, national authorities, and international investors. It applies multilingual large language models to measure stakeholder sentiment and uses topic modeling to identify the narratives underlying these perceptions. The proposed analysis investigates how stakeholder incentives and economic conditions shape attitudes toward the IMF and how these attitudes may affect policy implementation. The project is in progress; completed empirical findings are not yet reported.
