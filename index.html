Expectation Sentiment and Consumption Fluctuation from the Covid Era: A Natural Language Processing Approach

How do economic expectations translate into consumer spending? Combining daily transaction data with sentiment measures from 1.2 million U.S. news articles, I study Pfizer’s November 2020 vaccine announcement using a regression discontinuity in time design. Spending rose by 3.2%, with distinct responses across consumption categories and income groups.

  • High-frequency expectations from news text
  • Causal evidence around the vaccine announcement
  • Heterogeneous responses across income groups
Read full abstract +

This paper explores fluctuations in consumer spending during the COVID-19 pandemic, focusing on the role of consumer expectations amid income reductions and heightened uncertainty. Leveraging daily transaction data and sentiment indices derived from 1.2 million U.S. news articles, the study constructs topic-specific sentiment measures to gauge shifts in public expectations. Using a regression discontinuity design centered on Pfizer’s vaccine announcement, the analysis reveals a 3.2% overall increase in spending, driven primarily by grocery and food delivery sectors. Subsequent Vector Autoregressive (VAR) analysis highlights the influence of public health expectations on spending for essential goods, while recreational spending remains largely unaffected. The results also uncover divergent patterns between income groups, with high-income consumers responding more to income expectations and low-income consumers reacting to unemployment expectations. These findings underscore the critical role of expectation sentiment in shaping consumption behavior during periods of crisis.

Leveraging ChatGPT to Analyze Heterogeneous Treatment Effects: The Effect of Pfizer’s COVID-19 Vaccine Announcement on U.S. Consumption

Accepted to the 2025 IAAE and PEA conferences

This paper uses embeddings of ChatGPT-generated text to identify sources of heterogeneous treatment effects. Applied to the vaccine announcement, the framework finds stronger consumption responses in cities with higher population density, higher incomes, and weaker healthcare infrastructure.

Read full abstract +

Traditional analyses of heterogeneous treatment effects often rely on pre-specified, structured numerical baseline indicators to test for significant differences in treatment effects across groups. This paper introduces a novel framework that utilizes word embeddings generated from ChatGPT’s textual outputs to identify factors driving significant heterogeneous treatment effects. Applying this approach within a regression discontinuity in time design, we examine the key determinants influencing the impact of Pfizer’s announcement of 90% efficacy for its first COVID-19 vaccine on weekly consumption patterns across U.S. cities during the pandemic’s first year. The announcement boosted public confidence in future economic conditions, thereby influencing current spending behavior. Our findings indicate that cities with high population density, elevated income levels, and weaker healthcare infrastructure experienced significantly stronger positive impacts. This study demonstrates the potential of combining innovative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, with textual information to advance causal inference methods in applied econometrics.

Balancing Act: A Multi-Stakeholder Analysis of IMF Perceptions Using LLMs

Work in progress · Proposal submitted to the 17th Annual Conference of PEIO

Using multilingual large language models and topic modeling, this project studies how domestic publics, national authorities, and international investors perceived the IMF’s 2018 Exceptional Access Program in Argentina. It examines how stakeholder narratives may shape policy implementation and program effectiveness.

Read full abstract +

This paper investigates how diverse stakeholders perceived the IMF’s 2018 Exceptional Access Program (EAP) in Argentina, with the aim of understanding how such perceptions influence policy outcomes and program effectiveness. Leveraging recent advances in natural language processing, the study applies large language models (LLMs) to perform multilingual sentiment analysis across a range of sources, capturing views from domestic publics, national authorities, and international investors. To uncover the drivers of these sentiments, the paper employs topic modeling to identify key themes shaping narratives around the IMF and the broader economic context. By addressing three core questions—how stakeholders perceive the economy and the IMF, what incentives shape these perceptions, and how these perceptions influence policy outcomes—the study offers a novel framework for evaluating IMF engagement in crisis contexts. Through rigorous data filtering and methodological controls for bias and multiple comparisons, the paper provides one of the first large-scale, LLM-based analyses of multi-stakeholder sentiment toward an IMF program. Findings will contribute to a deeper understanding of the political economy dynamics that underpin IMF-supported reforms, with broader implications for global financial governance and stakeholder-informed policy design.
